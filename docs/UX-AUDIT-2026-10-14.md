# FarmFlow — UI/UX audit

**Date: 2026-10-14. Method: live application, real browser.** Audit only; no application code was
modified.

Unlike a source-only review, this audit drove the running app. I started the dev server against the
**dev** database, logged in as the owner account, entered the estate workspace through *Preview as
Estate Admin*, and measured 8 workspace tabs plus the login page and Owner Console at desktop
(1440×900) and phone (390×844) viewports, capturing screenshots, console output, computed contrast,
accessible names, touch-target sizes and layout overflow at each stop. Playwright's `tab-smoke` and
`public-flows` suites were also run and both passed.

**Two of my own automated findings were false positives and were withdrawn after checking.** They
are documented in §I rather than deleted, because the reason each was wrong constrains how much
weight the remaining numbers carry.

---

## A. Executive summary

**UX maturity: competent and coherent underneath, undermined by surface inconsistency that makes it
read as less finished than it is.**

The foundations are better than the screenshots suggest. The navigation model in
[`components/inventory-system/tab-items.ts`](../components/inventory-system/tab-items.ts) is a clean,
pure, module-gated function producing two sensible groups (Operations / Insights) with plain-English
labels — "Muster", "Costs", "Stock & Inventory", "Rain & Weather" are exactly the words a Coorg
estate uses. Breadcrumbs exist. Loading skeletons exist. The Costs tab distinguishes a real zero
from absent data correctly ("₹0 — 0 labour records in range"). The daily-action affordance on the
home screen (Labour / Expense / Rainfall as three large cards) is the right idea, well executed.
Nothing crashed, no page overflowed horizontally at either viewport, and every tab I reached
rendered.

**The problem is that three or four different navigation surfaces each name the same destinations
differently, and they are visible at the same time.** That single defect does more damage to
perceived quality than every other finding combined, because it is on screen continuously and it
makes the product feel like several products.

### The five highest-impact changes

1. **Give every destination one name.** Nine of seventeen destinations currently have two or three
   names rendered simultaneously — the sidebar says "Weather", the tab strip beside it says
   "Rain & Weather", the home card says "Rain & Weather", the phone says something else again.
   (UX-01)
2. **Fix the sidebar sub-navigation collision.** On Costs, the nested sub-items render *on top of*
   the rail item below them — "Dashboard" overlaps "Picking" — at 8px with emoji icons. This is the
   single worst-looking thing in the product and it is in the primary navigation. (UX-03, UX-04)
3. **Reclaim the fold.** Three stacked navigation levels plus banners push the Muster's first data
   row to roughly y=750 of a 900px viewport, and the primary action below it is cut off. The muster
   is the one screen used every morning by every tenant. (UX-05)
4. **Unblock analytics.** Google Analytics' beacon is refused by CSP on every page load, so the
   product is collecting no GA data at all. This is the *third* instance of a bug class CLAUDE.md
   already documents twice. (UX-02)
5. **Raise the two readability floors**: 8px sub-tab labels at 3.77:1, and the login page's
   supporting copy at 2.45:1 — which includes the "Create your estate" link, the entry point of the
   entire acquisition funnel. (UX-06, UX-07)

None of the five is architectural. All are a day or less each.

### UX-01 · P1 · Confirmed · One destination, three names, all on screen at once

**Module:** Global navigation · **Route:** `/dashboard` ·
**Files:** `components/inventory-system/tab-items.ts`, the sidebar rail component, `mobile-home-section.tsx`

**Observed.** The desktop sidebar, the horizontal tab strip, the home-screen cards and the phone
bottom bar each name the same destinations differently, and the first two are side by side in every
screenshot:

| Destination | Sidebar rail | Tab strip / home card | Phone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home | **Dashboard** | — | **Home** |
| Rainfall | **Weather** | **Rain & Weather** | — |
| Inventory | **Inventory** | **Stock & Inventory** | — |
| Picking | **Picking** | **Picking Log** | — |
| Balance sheet | **Financial Summary** | **Live Balance** | — |
| P&L | **Profit & Loss** | **P&L Report** | — |
| Season | **This Season** | **Season Summary** | — |
| AI | **AI** | **AI Insights** | — |
| News | **News** | **Market News** | — |
| Audit | **Activity Log** | **Audit Log** | — |
| Daily labour action | — | **Labour** / "Workers & wages" | **Log Muster** |
| Daily expense action | — | **Expense** / "Costs & inputs" | **Other Expense** |

**Evidence.** `shots/03-home.png` and `shots/tab-muster.png` show both columns in one frame. The
enumeration came from the live accessible-button list, not from source.

**Note:** `tab-items.ts` already carries a comment recording that "the desktop rail and the phone's
bottom nav were reconciled earlier today; this tab strip was the third surface naming the same tab"
— for **Costs**. That reconciliation was done once, for one tab, and the other nine were left.

**Users affected.** All, continuously. Worst for the data-entry user and field supervisor, who are
the least able to assume two words mean one thing.

**Business impact.** This is the main reason the product reads as unfinished. It also makes support
and training harder ("go to Weather" / "I don't have Weather, I have Rain & Weather") and makes
written SOPs ambiguous.

**Fix.** Make `tab-items.ts` the single source of the label and have the rail, the strip, the home
cards and the phone bar all read from it. The labels in `tab-items.ts` are the better set — keep
those. **Acceptance:** a test asserts that for every destination, the string rendered by all four
surfaces is identical, derived from the tab list rather than a hand-kept map. **Effort: S.**
**Regression risk:** low; e2e specs selecting by the old names will need updating — that is the
check working.

### UX-02 · P1 · Confirmed · Google Analytics is blocked by CSP on every page load

**Module:** Observability · **Files:** `app/layout.tsx:99-122`, `lib/csp.mjs:18-27`

**Observed.** Every page produced console errors:

```
Fetch API cannot load https://region1.google-analytics.com/g/collect?v=2&tid=G-X0RB06WXE9…
```

on 6 of 8 desktop tabs and all 3 phone tabs. `app/layout.tsx` loads `gtag.js` and configures
`G-X0RB06WXE9`. `CONNECT_SRC` allows `https://www.google-analytics.com` and
`https://www.googletagmanager.com` — but GA sends its beacon to the **regional** host
`region1.google-analytics.com`, which neither entry matches. The script loads; the data never
leaves.

**This is the third instance of one bug class in this file.** `lib/csp.mjs` already carries comments
about the malformed `https://o*.ingest.sentry.io` wildcard that meant client-side Sentry reported
nothing for months, and about Vercel Analytics being rendered on every page while CSP refused the
script. `tests/csp.test.ts` exists and passes, so it does not cover regional hosts.

**Business impact.** Every GA-derived number about acquisition is empty or wrong, which matters
precisely when marketing is about to be turned up. Also permanent console noise on every screen,
which buries real errors. (PostHog is separately configured and correctly allow-listed, so analytics
are not wholly absent — check whether GA is wanted at all before fixing it.)

**Fix.** Either delete the GA tag (PostHog already covers this) or add
`https://*.google-analytics.com` to `CONNECT_SRC`. **Acceptance:** a case in `tests/csp.test.ts`
asserting a regional host is permitted; manual check that `/g/collect` returns 2xx.
**Effort: S.** Google is already named on `/legal/subprocessors`, so there is no disclosure gap —
I checked.

### UX-03 · P1 · Confirmed · Sidebar sub-navigation renders on top of the rail items below it

**Module:** Global navigation · **Route:** `/dashboard` (Costs; likely every tab with sub-tabs)

**Observed.** With Costs active, the rail expands a nested list — `📊 Dashboard`, `👷 Labour`,
`💸 Non-Labour Expenses`, `🏷️ Codes`, `📤 Export` — at **8px**, and it visibly **collides with the
"Picking" rail item below**: the sub-item text overlaps the Picking icon and label.

**Evidence.** `shots/tab-costs.png`, left rail. Measured contrast of those labels: **3.77:1**
against a 4.5:1 requirement, at 8px.

**Business impact.** The primary navigation of a financial product has visibly overlapping text.
This is the single clearest "unfinished software" signal in the UI and sits on the screen used most.
For a prospect being demoed, it undermines trust in the numbers.

**Fix.** Give the expanded sub-list its own flow space (push the rail items down) or move it out of
the rail entirely — the sub-navigation already exists twice elsewhere (UX-04), so the rail copy may
simply be removable. Minimum type size 12px. **Acceptance:** at 1440×900 and 1280×720, no rail
element's bounding box intersects another's; all rail labels ≥12px and ≥4.5:1.
**Effort: S.** **Regression risk:** low.

### UX-04 · P1 · Confirmed · The same sub-navigation is rendered twice, with different labels, and partly obscured

**Module:** Costs (and, by the same pattern, Dispatch and Sales) · **Route:** `/dashboard`

**Observed.** On Costs, five sub-destinations appear **in the sidebar** (`📊 Dashboard / 👷 Labour /
💸 Non-Labour Expenses / 🏷️ Codes / 📤 Export`, 8px, emoji) **and again in a floating bottom bar**
(`Dashboard / Labour / Non-Labour Expenses / Activity Codes / Export`, normal size, lucide icons).
Two differences matter:

- The labels disagree: **"Codes" vs "Activity Codes"**.
- The bottom bar's first item is **overlapped by a "FARMFLOW" watermark badge**, so "Dashboard"
  renders as "…rd".

**Evidence.** `shots/tab-costs.png`, bottom edge and left rail. The same watermark sits at the same
coordinates on `03-home.png` and `tab-muster.png`.

**Business impact.** Users cannot tell whether the two lists do the same thing. The clipped first
item looks like a rendering fault.

**Fix.** Keep one sub-navigation — the bottom bar is the legible one. Re-position the watermark or
remove it from the workspace. **Acceptance:** one sub-navigation per tab; no element overlaps the
first sub-nav item; labels match `tab-items.ts` `subtabs`. **Effort: S.**

### UX-05 · P1 · Confirmed · Three stacked navigation levels push the muster below the fold

**Module:** Muster · **Route:** `/dashboard` → Muster

**Observed.** At 1440×900, vertical space before the first worker row on the Muster:

| Band | approx. height |
|---|---|
| Header (breadcrumb, search, theme, help, support, avatar) | 95px |
| Preview banner * | 180px |
| Sync bar | 45px |
| Section header ("OPERATIONS / Muster & work allocation") | 55px |
| Horizontal tab strip (8 items) | 60px |
| Sub-tab strip (Muster / Workers / Payroll / Attendance reports / Scanner) | 60px |
| Week selector | 110px |
| Table header | 30px |
| **First worker row** | **≈ y=750** |

The primary action, **"Add employee or crew", is cut off at the bottom edge.**

\* The preview banner is owner-only and a real admin would not see it — subtract 180px and the first
row still lands at ≈570, with roughly two rows visible before scrolling.

**Evidence.** `shots/tab-muster.png`.

**Business impact.** The muster is marked in the morning, often on a laptop in an office, and is the
single most-used screen across all four live tenants. Every row costs a scroll.

**Fix.** Collapse the section header into the breadcrumb (they say the same thing — "Muster" appears
three times above the fold); make the Sync bar part of the header; consider whether the horizontal
tab strip is needed at all when the rail is present. **Acceptance:** at 1366×768 with no preview
banner, at least five worker rows and the "Add employee or crew" action are visible without
scrolling. **Effort: M.** **Regression risk:** medium — layout changes affect every tab.

### UX-06 · P2 · Confirmed · Login page supporting copy fails contrast, including the signup link

**Route:** `/login` · **Measured on a solid background, so the number is reliable.**

| Text | Ratio | Required | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| "No login yet? Create your estate." | **2.45** | 4.5 | 12px |
| "You will stay signed in for 30 days." | **2.45** | 4.5 | 11px |
| "By signing in, you acknowledge the Privacy Notice." | **2.45** | 4.5 | 12px |
| "First time here? Create your estate…" | **2.45** | 4.5 | 12px |

**Business impact.** This is the first screen every user sees, and two of the four strings are the
**signup entry point** — the top of the only acquisition funnel the product has. It is also the
screen most likely to be viewed on a phone in daylight.

**Fix.** Raise the muted foreground for these strings. Note `project_stone_400_contrast_decision`
resolved a related case at `--muted-foreground/0.8` (4.58:1) and explicitly warned against extending
it to `stone-300` — so check which token these use rather than reusing that fix blindly.
**Acceptance:** every text node on `/login` ≥4.5:1 (≥3:1 for ≥24px), asserted in a render test.
**Effort: S.**

### UX-07 · P2 · Confirmed · 8px sub-tab labels at 3.77:1, in three modules

**Modules:** Costs, Dispatch, Sales

Measured labels at **8px / 3.77:1**: `📊 Dashboard`, `👷 Labour`, `💸 Non-Labour Expenses`,
`🏷️ Codes`, `📤 Export`, `📦 Stock Flow`, `🚚 New Dispatch`, `📋 Records`, `📊 Overview`,

the sub-nav is removed. **Acceptance:** no interactive label below 12px anywhere in the workspace,
enforced by a render test. **Effort: S.**

### UX-09 · P2 · Confirmed · Mobile navigation truncates and renames

**Observed.** The phone bottom bar reads `Home | Muster | Processi… | Costs | More` — "Processing"
is **truncated with an ellipsis**. "Home" here is "Dashboard" on desktop. The three quick-action
cards are "Log Muster / Other Expense / Rainfall" on the phone and "Labour / Expense / Rainfall" on

short form used on both). **Acceptance:** no bottom-nav label truncates at 360px; quick-action
labels identical across viewports. **Effort: S.**

### UX-10 · P2 · Confirmed · Two icon systems, split by surface

Emoji (👷 💸 🏷️ 📤 📦 🚚 📋 💰 📊) are used in sub-tab labels and on the phone's quick-action cards;
lucide line icons are used in the rail, the tab strip, the home cards and the desktop bottom bar.
The *same three cards* render emoji on the phone and SVG on desktop.

Emoji also render differently per OS and per Android vendor, so the phone UI's appearance is outside
the team's control.

**Fix.** Lucide everywhere. **Acceptance:** no emoji in navigation or control labels.
**Effort: S.**

### UX-11 · P2 · Confirmed · Zero and "no data yet" are treated inconsistently for the same metric

**Observed.** The Costs tab does this **correctly**: "₹0" with the qualifier "0 labour records in
range" and "0 expense records in range", plus "FY 26/27 — April 1 to March 31 reporting window". A
reader knows the figure is real and what window it covers.

The **phone home hero** shows the same money as a bare "**₹0**" under "TOTAL COSTS THIS FY", with
"Labour ₹0 / Expenses ₹0" and "RAINFALL (2026) 0 in" and no record count. On a new estate this is
indistinguishable from a failure.

**Evidence.** `shots/mob-01-home.png` vs `shots/tab-costs.png`.

**Business impact.** Directly relevant to onboarding: the first thing a new estate owner sees on
their phone is ₹0 with no explanation. It is also the UI half of a defect class this codebase has
documented at the data layer (`lib/server/incomplete-read.ts`) — the server now refuses to report a
failed read as zero, and the phone home does not yet carry that distinction through to the user.

**Fix.** Carry the record count (or an explicit "No records yet — add your first") into the hero.
**Acceptance:** on a tenant with zero records every money tile states the record count or an empty
state; on a tenant with records the figure is shown. **Effort: S.**

### UX-12 · P2 · Confirmed · The Owner Console has no heading structure and no real tabs

**Route:** `/admin/tenants`

**Observed.** A live DOM query found **zero visible `h1`–`h6` elements** on the entire page, and

**Fix.** A single `h1`, section `h2`s, and the Radix tabs primitive already used elsewhere.
**Acceptance:** exactly one `h1`; `role="tablist"`/`tab`/`tabpanel` present; arrow keys move between
tabs. **Effort: M.**

**Route:** `/admin/tenants` → System Health

### UX-15 · P2 · Confirmed · `pnpm test:e2e:auth` cannot run — the script it calls does not exist

**File:** `package.json` → `test:e2e:auth` calls `node scripts/dev/check-e2e-auth-env.mjs`, which is
**absent from the repository**. Running it gives `Error: Cannot find module …check-e2e-auth-env.mjs`.

Seven specs are behind that entry point — `dashboard-regression`, `owner-console`, `tab-smoke`,

I confirmed the specs themselves are fine by invoking Playwright directly: `tab-smoke` passed in
46.6s.

**Business impact.** This is plausibly *why* findings UX-01 through UX-05 survive: the suite that
would catch UI regressions is not being run, and it fails in a way that looks like an environment
problem rather than a missing file.

**Fix.** Restore or remove the pre-flight check. **Acceptance:** `pnpm test:e2e:auth` runs to a pass
or a clear "credentials not set" message. **Effort: S.**

### UX-17 · P2 · Confirmed (design judgement) · The home screen is a second copy of the navigation

**Route:** `/dashboard` → Home

Below the hero, the home screen renders "OPERATIONS: Muster · Costs · Picking Log · Processing ·
Dispatch · Sales · Stock & Inventory · Rain & Weather" and "INSIGHTS: Live Balance · P&L Report ·
Season Summary · AI Insights" as cards — **the same destinations already in the rail two inches to
the left**, with no counts, no status, no values.

The genuinely useful parts are the hero (estate name, date, a contextual "Harvest prep" note) and
the three "LOG TODAY" cards. Everything below is a menu.

This is the evidence for product question 3 (§11): the dashboard currently tells an owner *where
things are*, not *what matters*. An owner who opens FarmFlow remotely learns nothing without
clicking.

**Fix.** Put state on the cards — "Muster · 12 marked today", "Costs · ₹1.2L this month, 3 unpriced
items", "Rain & Weather · no reading for 4 days". The data already exists behind
`/api/dashboard/estate-pulse`, `hints` and `intelligence-brief`. **Acceptance:** every home card
shows at least one live figure or an explicit empty state. **Effort: M.** This is the single
highest-value *product* change in the report, as opposed to the highest-value *fix*.

### UX-22 · P2 · Confirmed · The horizontal tab strip disappears once you leave Home

**Observed.** My probe failed to reach 9 of 17 tabs with "nav item absent" — because after
navigating into a tab, the buttons named "Stock & Inventory", "Live Balance", "P&L Report",
"Season Summary", "AI Insights", "Market News", "Audit Log" and "Picking Log" were no longer in the

for no stated reason.

**Fix.** Render both groups in the strip, or drop the strip and rely on the rail.
**Acceptance:** every tab visible to a tenant is reachable in one click from every other tab.
**Effort: S.**

## E. UX consistency report

**Repeated patterns that work and should be the standard:**
- Section header + workspace card + KPI tile row (Costs, Dispatch, Sales) is a coherent, repeatable
  module template.
- `tab-items.ts` as a pure, testable navigation model is the right architecture; the inconsistency
  is that three surfaces don't read from it.
- The Costs KPI tiles' "₹0 — 0 labour records in range" is the best empty-state pattern in the
  product. Adopt it everywhere money is shown.

**Inconsistencies to resolve, in order:**

| # | Pattern | Variants found | Recommended standard |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Destination naming | up to 3 per destination | `tab-items.ts` label, read by all surfaces |
| 2 | Iconography | lucide SVG, emoji | lucide only |
| 3 | Sub-navigation placement | sidebar nest + floating bottom bar | one, below the workspace header |
| 4 | Zero vs missing | "₹0 + 0 records" vs bare "₹0" | always qualify |
| 5 | Date/time rendering | "14-Oct-2026, 2:24 PM", "2:25:46 PM", raw ISO | `lib/date-utils.ts` helpers only |
| 6 | Page title | breadcrumb + rail + section header + card title | breadcrumb + one heading |
| 7 | Assistant entry | two floating buttons | one |
| 8 | Minimum type size | 8px, 9px, 10px, 11px, 12px+ | 12px floor for anything interactive |

**Duplicated functionality:** the home screen's Operations/Insights card grid duplicates the rail
(UX-17); the sub-navigation renders twice (UX-04); `tableHasLocationColumn` is duplicated in four
API routes (carried from the production audit, §C of `AUDIT-2026-10-14.md`).

## I. Limitations — what I could not verify, and the two findings I withdrew

### Two withdrawn false positives

**Withdrawn: "catastrophic contrast failures on the hero and quick-action cards."** My first probe
reported ratios of **1.04–1.20:1** for "Kodagu Test Estate", "₹0", "Labour", "Expense", "Rainfall"
and others. All were artifacts: those elements sit on **CSS gradients**, which
`getComputedStyle().backgroundColor` reports as transparent, so my background walk climbed past the
gradient to the near-white page background and computed white-on-white. The screenshots show white
text on dark green, orange and blue — legible. I rewrote the check to detect
`backgroundImage !== "none"` and report such text as **unmeasurable** rather than failing; the
re-run found **9–25 unmeasurable elements per tab** and a much smaller set of genuine failures,
which is what §C reports. **Automated contrast tooling will make this same mistake on this
codebase** — gradients are used heavily — so any contrast test added must carry the same exclusion.

**Withdrawn: "the FarmFlow logo button has no accessible name," reported on 8 tabs.** The button
contains `<img alt="FarmFlow">`, which supplies the name. My accessible-name check did not consider
image `alt` text on child elements. Not a defect.

### Not verified

- **Every write workflow.** I submitted no form, edited no record, deleted nothing. So §4 of the
  brief — click counts, defaults, inline validation, duplicate submission, unsaved-change loss,
  save confirmations, insufficient-stock handling, edit-without-inconsistency — is **unverified**.
  The brief asked me not to invent click counts, so §D is largely empty rather than estimated.
- **Error, offline and permission states.** Not one was triggered. No failed API call, no dropped
  connection, no concurrent edit, no permission denial, no partial success.
- **9 of 17 workspace tabs**, including Stock & Inventory on desktop, Rain & Weather, Live Balance,
  P&L Report, Season Summary, AI Insights and Audit Log.
- **All of Settings, all marketing pages, all legal pages, `/welcome`, `/trial-expired`, `/offline`,
  the import flow, and the self-serve onboarding journey** — the last being the most commercially
  important omission, given it is the funnel.
- **Manager and writer roles.** Only the owner account exists locally, and I viewed the workspace
  through *Preview as Estate Admin*, which the banner itself describes as a UI/module preview. A
  real admin or writer session may differ.
- **Populated data.** The test tenant has one worker and zero money, so dense tables, pagination,
  long worker/location dropdowns, totals, horizontal scroll and chart rendering with real values are
  all unverified. UX-16 cannot be resolved without this.
- **Keyboard-only navigation, visible focus states, and screen readers.** I measured structure
  (headings, roles, accessible names) but drove nothing by keyboard and ran no screen reader.
- **Tablet viewports, browser zoom, text scaling, and any browser other than Chromium.**
- **Light/dark parity.** Emulating `prefers-color-scheme: dark` produced identical measurements,
  which indicates the app switches theme by its own toggle rather than the media query — so my dark
  pass did not actually test dark mode.
- **Real-device performance.** Everything was local against a dev server; perceived speed on a
  mid-range Android over rural mobile data is untested and is the condition that matters most.

### One operational note

Loading `.env.local` into the shell to run the authenticated browser session caused bash's
job-completion notice to echo the **dev** `APP_DATABASE_URL` into this terminal session several
times. It was not written to any file, committed, or transmitted. It is the dev database, not
production. Rotating that `app_runtime` dev credential is cheap insurance if you would rather not
rely on that.

## J. Proposed redesign — only where the evidence supports it

Three changes, each tied to a finding above. I am deliberately **not** proposing a visual redesign:
the typography, spacing and card system are already coherent and professional, and the problems are
structural and lexical rather than aesthetic.

### 1. One navigation, not three

Today: a rail (17 items, one naming scheme), a horizontal strip (8 items, Operations only, a second
naming scheme), home cards (a third copy), a sub-nav rendered twice, and a phone bar with a fourth
scheme.

Proposed: **the rail is the navigation.** Grouped Operations / Insights as now, using the
`tab-items.ts` labels. Drop the horizontal tab strip (it duplicates the rail and only carries half
the destinations — UX-22). Keep **one** sub-navigation, directly under the workspace header, with
lucide icons at 12px+. On the phone, the bottom bar keeps 4 destinations plus "More", with labels
that fit.

Net effect: roughly 120px of vertical space returned on every tab, one naming scheme, and the
UX-03 collision disappears because the rail no longer nests.

### 2. The home screen becomes a status board

Keep the hero and the three "LOG TODAY" cards — both are good and the daily-entry affordance is the
best thing on the screen. Replace the Operations/Insights card grid, which is a duplicate menu, with:

- **Needs attention** — the things only a computer would notice: unpriced stock leaving the store,
  a worker booked over one day, no rainfall reading in N days, workers with no rate. These checks
  already exist as `scripts/dev/` harnesses and `/api/dashboard/hints`.
- **This week** — muster days marked, labour cost, expenses, with last week beside them.
- **Season** — only during season: picked, processed, dispatched, sold.

Each tile links to the tab that explains it. An owner then learns something in the first five
seconds, which is what §11.3 and §11.4 are really asking.

### 3. A small set of enforced shared rules

Not a design system rebuild — four rules with tests behind them, because this codebase's own history
shows that an unenforced convention regresses:

1. 12px minimum for any interactive label.
2. 4.5:1 minimum on solid backgrounds; gradients explicitly excluded and reviewed by eye.
3. Lucide icons only; no emoji in navigation or controls.
4. Money is never rendered without either a record count or an explicit empty state.
